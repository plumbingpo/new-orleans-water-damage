Water Damage We See Most in Lafayette

Water damage does not always arrive as a sudden gush across the kitchen. Here is what homeowners around Lafayette phone us about most, along with what usually sits behind each one.

Hidden leaks: Small drips from a worn fitting or a tiny hole in a pipe may go unnoticed behind drywall for a long time. Watch for a water bill that climbs for no reason, soft spots on the floor, and paint that blisters. Thermal imaging and moisture readings let us map how far it spread before anything gets cut open. Even a slow leak matters, because wood and drywall that stay damp will rot and grow mold.

Sewage: When a main drain line clogs, wastewater comes back up through floor drains, tubs and toilets on the lowest level. Tree roots, grease buildup and heavy rain overloading the public system are the common triggers. Sewage counts as Category 3, the most contaminated class, and it is not safe to clean up bare-handed. Carpet, padding and other absorbent materials that got wet usually cannot be saved.

Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. It enters at door thresholds, low windows and the garage, wherever the ground slopes toward the building. Because surface water carries dirt and chemicals from the ground it passed over, it is handled as unsanitary. Homes across Lafayette can see this in any season with a strong enough storm.

Crawlspace: In homes built over a crawl space, few people look underneath, so moisture there builds up unseen. Standing water, a torn vapor barrier or a leaking pipe overhead keeps the air below your floors humid. Over time that leads to sagging insulation, musty odors indoors and soft or cupped flooring. We pump out what has collected, dry the joists and subfloor, and show you where the moisture is coming from.

Condensate: Cooling equipment produces a steady stream of condensation whenever it runs in damp weather. It is meant to exit through a narrow drain tube, and that tube plugs up with slime and debris. A blockage makes the drip pan spill over, wetting the ceiling or floor near the indoor unit. Most owners learn of it from a ceiling stain or drooping drywall, so have the drain line cleared once a year.

The First Hour After Water Damage in Lafayette

People mean well in the first hour after a leak, and some of what they do makes things worse. Here are the slip-ups we run into most on Lafayette jobs, with the better option for each.

Never run an ordinary vacuum cleaner over a wet floor. You risk a shock and a dead vacuum, and a mop with old towels does the job safely. Leave ceiling fixtures off if the ceiling above is wet, and keep away from any part that droops. Do not lift tacked-down carpet yourself, since it can shrink or tear and may be saveable if it is handled properly. Avoid cranking up the heating, as warmth plus moisture encourages mold.

Do shut off the water, open cabinet doors and closets so air can reach them, and hang up wet fabrics and cushions to drip. Photograph each room before you shift a single item. Keep kids and animals out until it has been checked. Most of all, phone early, so a New Orleans Restoration crew is already heading for Lafayette while you work through this list.

Simple Ways to Keep a Lafayette Home Dry

A flood in the house feels like it came from nowhere, yet most trace back to wear that built up over months or years. For homeowners in Lafayette, prevention is mainly a matter of knowing where to look.

Pipes and fittings top the list. A water bill that climbs for no reason, or pressure that seems too high, can signal a concealed leak or strain on the system. Next come appliances. Run the dishwasher and the washer only while someone is in the house, and change their hoses before they show cracks. The roof and the ground outside are third. Missing shingles, cracked flashing, blocked gutters and soil that slopes toward the house all let rain find a way in.

Going away for more than a few days? Close the main valve before you go, or have a neighbor look in. Homeowners in Lafayette can ask New Orleans Restoration about any of this. If something does fail, calling quickly is what keeps the damage contained.
