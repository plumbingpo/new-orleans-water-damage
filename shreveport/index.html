Common Water Damage Issues in Shreveport

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for Shreveport households. None of them is rare, and each gets worse the longer the water sits.

Mold: Mold is usually the second chapter of a water problem that someone thought was finished. Fans and towels dry what you can see, while the wall cavity, subfloor and carpet pad stay damp underneath. Since colonies can form in 24 to 48 hours, the first hint is often an earthy smell or speckled drywall weeks later.

Hoses: The braided or rubber lines behind toilets, sinks and washing machines are under pressure all day. A burst connector keeps spraying until somebody closes a valve, and that may take a long while in an empty house. The low-cost defense is to swap out aging hoses and learn where the main shutoff is.

Pumps: Sump pumps, where a home has one, tend to fail at the worst moment after years of running without attention. What we usually see is a jammed float, a motor that has worn out or a breaker that tripped. That leaves the lowest level under a few inches of water, with flooring, wall bases and stored belongings soaked. Testing the pump now and then, and adding a battery backup, lowers the odds considerably.

Toilets: Among bathroom emergencies in Shreveport, the toilet that will not stop rising tops the list. Clean water from the tank or supply is one thing, but water from the bowl after use is contaminated. Either way it moves under tile edges and vanity cabinets, so the floor needs to be checked with a meter.

Cooling: Few people think about the small pipe that carries condensation away from an AC system. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. Because it happens slowly, the damage in many Shreveport homes is well along before a stain appears. Having the line flushed each year, before the cooling season, prevents most of these.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. Garages, walkout basements and ground-floor rooms at the low side of the lot flood first. Our New Orleans Restoration crews extract it, remove what cannot be cleaned and dry the structure to verified readings.
