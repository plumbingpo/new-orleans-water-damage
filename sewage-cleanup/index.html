A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across New Orleans, New Orleans Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.
